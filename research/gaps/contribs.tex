\section{Candidate contributions and how they survive review}

Six candidate contributions were derived from the themes above. Each was
required to name an experiment, a baseline and a metric before it was allowed
through. Each was then to be handed to two hostile reviewers instructed to
default to refuted and to cite only prior work they could find live.

That adversarial pass completed for two of the six before the run was cut
short by a resource limit. Both of the two were refuted. This is reported
exactly as it happened, because the pattern is informative: the two claims that
were tested are the two that sound most like the product pitch, and both were
already occupied. The four that remain untested carry the author's own risk
assessment instead, which names the specific prior work a reviewer would reach
for. Treat an untested claim as unproven rather than as surviving.

\subsection{Candidate 1: deadline-bounded peer querying}

\textbf{The claim.} Correctness for an inter-agent query is a joint predicate
over answer quality and a wall clock deadline. Mytheus instantiates it as an
addressed request envelope carrying \texttt{requester\_agent\_id},
\texttt{responder\_target}, \texttt{query}, \texttt{deadline} and
\texttt{correlation\_id}, evaluated deny-by-default on tenant plus role at the
receive path, with a fixed four tier fallback ordered by evidence authority:
peer participant agents, then agents of related meetings, then retrieval, then
silence. Two properties are unrepresentable in every framework in the corpus.
Degradation runs \emph{downward} across evidence sources when the clock binds,
rather than upward into more serial model calls. And silence is a scored
terminal state rather than a defect.

\textbf{The gap it exploits.} Themes 1 and 3. Papers 1, 2 and 5 terminate on a
condition or a cap and never on a clock. Paper 3 finds no temporal failure mode
across 1642 traces and classes withholding and early stopping as pure defects.
Paper 6 states that agents coordinate poorly in real time and that its
subagents never query one another.

\textbf{Mechanism.} \texttt{ContextGapAgent} over the
\texttt{agent-requests} and \texttt{agent-responses} protocol, with per round
trip logging and the tenant plus role check on the receive path.

\textbf{Evaluation.} Replay the AMI scenario series, four linked design
meetings per team with four fixed roles, as real audio through the live capture
path so that recognition and diarization error are present. Construct
context-gap items by withholding meeting $k-1$ from one participant's scope, so
the gold fill is a prior-meeting fact held by exactly one peer. Baselines over
the identical window stream: an AutoGen GroupChat orchestrator terminated by
max rounds; always-RAG with no peer tier; broadcast-and-first-reply; the
Mytheus ladder; and an oracle with unbounded time. Report fill correctness,
citation validity, p50 and p95 round trip, deadline miss rate, the fraction of
fills served by each tier with per tier correctness, silence precision, and
dollars per meeting. The falsifiable hypothesis is that the count-terminated
baseline either exceeds the budget or terminates before the fill arrives.

\textbf{Adversarial review: not yet run.} The author's own assessment names the
attack. A reviewer will say the mechanism is ordinary distributed systems
engineering, since deadline propagation, hedged requests and fallback chains
are standard practice, and deadline-triggered quality degradation is formalised
in the anytime algorithm and flexible computation literature that sits behind
paper 23. LangGraph and AutoGen both permit a timeout as configuration, so
``no framework can express it'' is attackable as a claim about defaults rather
than expressiveness.

\begin{takeaway}
\textbf{Claim the clock, not the topology.} The defensible residue is narrower
than the ladder itself: the measurement under a live speech deadline, the
multi-principal authorization on the receive path, and silence as a scored
outcome. State plainly that five of the six agents are workflows in paper 5's
sense, and do not claim the topology as novel.
\end{takeaway}

\subsection{Candidate 2: utterance-level citation validity}

\textbf{The claim.} When the cited unit is a speaker-attributed utterance,
validity decomposes into three separable components: the span resolves, the
span entails the statement, and the span is attributed to the correct speaker.
These are empirically independent, so a system can hold near-constant
entailment-based citation precision while misattributing a large fraction of
commitments.

\textbf{Adversarial review: refuted.} The reviewer found the core empirical
claim already published, on the same corpora. Both sources below were
independently verified against the arXiv API for this document.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{M3-SLU: Evaluating Speaker-Attributed Reasoning in Multimodal
Large Language Models}, arXiv 2510.19358, built from CHiME-6, MELD,
MultiDialog and AMI. Its headline finding is that models capture what was said
but often fail to identify who said it. That is the claimed independence
result, stated as the paper's thesis.
\item \textbf{MSU-Bench: Towards Speaker-Centric Understanding in
Conversational Multi-Speaker Scenarios}, arXiv 2606.22868. It deliberately
varies speaker referencing schemes in order to disentangle content
understanding errors from speaker localization errors, and reports wrong
speaker attribution as a measured fraction of errors.
\end{itemize}

The author had pre-emptively retreated to ``what survives is the empirical
independence result''. That retreat is precisely what the search killed.

\textbf{What survives.} A narrower measurement note, not a decomposition
claim. On decision-typed and action-typed commitments extracted from diarized
transcripts, entailment-based citation precision is near-invariant to speaker
binding errors: holding the cited chunk fixed and permuting only the
\texttt{speaker} field leaves the entailment verdict, and therefore the
reported precision, essentially unchanged, while the fraction of commitments
bound to the wrong committer moves over a wide range. M3-SLU and MSU-Bench
measure speaker attribution through question answering accuracy, not through a
citation metric, so neither demonstrates the invariance of the citation metric
itself. Run it as the speaker-permutation ablation inside candidate 5 rather
than as a standalone paper.

\subsection{Candidate 3: unqueried role-conditioned relevance}

\textbf{The claim.} Score a shared 30 second window for a participant who has
issued no query and co-authored only part of the input, conditioned on
structured organizational attributes rather than behavioural history, and test
it against the null that per-participant divergence is annotation noise.

\textbf{Adversarial review: refuted, and this is the most important finding in
the document.} The reviewer reported that the mechanism is anticipated
component for component by an issued patent, \textbf{US11271978B2, Personalized
meeting summaries}, together with a second patent, US12272362B2, covering near
real time per attendee content ranking. According to the reviewer's fetched
claim text, the first determines role information of the participant built from
job title, responsibilities and projects, computes a relevance value against a
relevance threshold, and operates in real time as the meeting progresses.

\textbf{An integrity caveat you must resolve before relying on this.} Every
academic citation in these two documents was verified against a live API. These
two patent numbers were \emph{not}: the patent databases returned 503 and
Cloudflare challenges to automated access, and the search budget was exhausted
before the check could be completed another way. The claim language above is
reported as the reviewer stated it, not as verified fact. Confirm both patents
by hand before designing around them, and treat a patent's existence as a
novelty problem for the mechanism claim rather than for the measurement claim.

\textbf{What survives.} Drop the mechanism claim and publish a measurement and
falsification result instead. Profile-conditioned relevance over shared meeting
input is deployed and, apparently, patented, but it has never been tested
against the null that its per-participant differences are noise. The two
missing controls are the contribution:

\begin{enumerate}
\item A chance-corrected inter-role agreement statistic over a role-conditioned
relevance reference set on the same meeting windows, reported \emph{before} any
quality number. If agreement is near unity, the product thesis is weakened, and
that is a result worth publishing either way.
\item A \textbf{swapped-profile control}, in which participant A's scorer is
driven by participant B's profile, with the matched minus swapped gap as the
personalization effect size. Targeted searching found no mismatched-profile or
placebo-profile condition anywhere in personalized summarization or personalized
meeting systems. This appears genuinely unpublished and it is cheap to run.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Candidate 4: confirmation-gated organizational memory}

\textbf{The claim.} Every index in the corpus is built by an offline batch pass
over a corpus complete before indexing begins, and the one system that revises
its record does so automatically with no confidence and no reported precision.
Mytheus evaluates the opposite contract: an agent emits only a candidate, and
the row, the event and the graph edge are written on user confirmation, so the
write path stays off the live budget and retraction is free. The claim is a
measured tradeoff surface, not a claim of superiority. Confirmation gating
should raise edge precision and lower write cost while lowering edge coverage,
and the size of each effect is unknown in the published record.

\textbf{Gap and mechanism.} Theme 6. Paper 14 makes automatic edge invalidation
its core mechanism, decided by a model with no confidence and no human
confirmation, and reports only downstream accuracy, never precision, recall or
false-invalidation rate. The mechanism is the Apache AGE property graph written
only on confirmation, over \texttt{PRODUCED}, \texttt{ASSIGNED\_TO},
\texttt{RELATES\_TO} and \texttt{FOLLOWS\_FROM}, with conflict detection as a
graph traversal.

\textbf{Evaluation.} On the AMI scenario series, human-label decision pairs
into five classes, with hard negatives as the point of the set: contradiction,
refinement, scope restriction, reversal with stated rationale, and unrelated.
Compare a GraphRAG-style extraction graph, a Zep-style automatic invalidation
policy, the Mytheus confirmation-gated graph, and prompt-only contradiction
detection as the ablation that tests whether traversal beats asking a model.
Report model calls and wall clock per ingested window and per confirmed edge,
edge precision against coverage, contradiction precision and recall at the
stated true base rate, false-invalidation rate, and traversal p95 as a function
of graph size.

\textbf{Adversarial review: not yet run.} The author's assessment: a reviewer
will say human curation trivially beats automatic extraction on precision, so
the headline is a foregone conclusion, and that cost-per-edge comparisons are
decided by whoever defines the unit. Human-in-the-loop knowledge graph
construction is an established line of work outside this corpus. There is also
a feasibility exposure, since AMI's staged decisions may be too clean to yield
realistic refinement and scope-restriction negatives. Mitigate by declaring
coverage loss up front as the price of the contract, and by making the hard
negative taxonomy, rather than the win, the released artifact.

\subsection{Candidate 5: the shelf life of a grounding verdict}

\textbf{The claim.} On a live stream the evidence beneath a judgement is
mutable, because interim recognition hypotheses are revised and only finals
persist, so an extraction, a confidence value and a citation can all be
computed against text that no longer exists by the time a user sees the
candidate. Define \emph{candidate retraction rate}, the fraction of emitted
candidates whose extraction, confidence band or citation does not survive the
interim to final transition, and \emph{confidence churn}, the signed change in
confidence between interim-derived and final-derived judgement, both as
functions of firing offset inside the 30 second window.

\textbf{Evaluation.} Fire \texttt{DecisionAgent}, \texttt{ActionAgent} and
\texttt{QuestionAgent} at offsets of 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 and 30 seconds into each
window and compare every candidate against a final-transcript oracle run on the
same window. Per offset, report retraction rate, band-flip rate for candidates
crossing the 0.7 or 0.4 cut, mean absolute confidence churn, citation
invalidation rate when \texttt{chunk\_id} or \texttt{speaker} changes on
finalization, extraction F1, and seconds of latency saved. The deliverable is
one curve, seconds saved against retraction rate, so an operating point can be
chosen rather than asserted.

\textbf{Adversarial review: not yet run.} The author's assessment is that
incremental natural language processing already formalised this shape, since
incremental processing metrics define correctness over time and edit overhead
for revised output, and streaming recognition has long reported instability of
emitted prefixes. Paper 17 already scores partial output over time. The residue
that survives both objections is that the revised object here is a
confidence-scored, speaker-cited structured extraction rather than a token
prefix, and that band-flip rate has no counterpart in that literature because
those systems have no gate to flip across. Fold the surviving fragment of
candidate 2 in here as the speaker-permutation ablation.

\subsection{Candidate 6: abstention priced across a tier cascade}

\textbf{The claim.} Every gate in this system is an abstention with a price,
and none is currently fitted. The triage pre-gate discards roughly 65 percent
of windows before any extraction call is paid for, which differs structurally
from a cascade: the decision precedes generation, the false negative is silent
and permanent rather than recoverable by escalation, volume is set by the clock
and headcount rather than by a query distribution, and the loss is incident on
the specific participants for whom the window mattered. The 0.7 and 0.4 bands
then define a three action decision that a two action risk-coverage curve
cannot express.

\textbf{Evaluation.} Sweep the triage threshold and plot per-role recall of
relevant windows against calls and dollars saved, reporting the silent
false-drop rate per role at the shipped operating point. Sweep both cut points
jointly and compare the asserted uniform 0.7 and 0.4 against per-agent, per-tier
cuts fitted by Platt scaling on reported-confidence against correctness pairs,
fitting on a tenant-level holdout rather than on the CI suite that gates
prompts. Report reliability diagrams for a per-person relevance predicate and a
shared checkable correctness predicate on the same scale, and report the
calibration penalty of forcing one shared cut.

\textbf{Adversarial review: not yet run.} This is the most crowded ground in
the list and should be presented as measurement hygiene rather than as a
headline. Selective prediction with a reject option is classical, the
calibration machinery is paper 18 plus standard scaling, and cheap-model-first
routing is paper 22 plus the router line. A reviewer can fairly say the only
new element is applying known calibration and known routing to a new pipeline.
Two pieces are genuinely unoccupied and should carry whatever novelty is
claimed: the per-role incidence of a silent, unrecoverable pre-gate, and the
demonstration that a relevance predicate and a correctness predicate cannot
share a cut point no matter how well each is calibrated separately.
