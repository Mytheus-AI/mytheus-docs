\section{What the literature does not cover}

Six independent analyses read all twenty five limitation sets, each hunting a
different kind of absence. They converged on six themes. What follows is
ordered by how countable the gap is, because a gap you can count across
twenty five papers is one a reviewer cannot wave away.

One framing result comes first, because it organises everything else. Sort the
corpus on two axes, whether the input is a static corpus or a live stream, and
whether the system owes one shared output or a different output per recipient.
Papers land in three quadrants. The fourth is empty.

The empty quadrant is not proof of novelty. It is proof that the two
constraints have never been imposed together, which is a weaker but far more
defensible statement, and it is the statement every theme below refines.

\subsection{Theme 1: the tail is never reported}

Not one of the twenty five papers reports a percentile latency for its own
method. This is the most countable gap in the corpus and the easiest to
defend, because it rests on an absence rather than an interpretation.

Efficiency is not ignored by this literature. It is simply always expressed as
a mean, a ratio or a token count, never as a distribution and never against a
deadline. Paper 13, HippoRAG, reports being six to thirteen times faster than
IRCoT but gives no absolute p50 or p95 and no tail curve as the graph grows.
Paper 12, From Local to Global, prices queries only in context tokens. Paper
22, FrugalGPT, never measures latency anywhere, despite a strictly sequential
cascade whose worked example queries every API in the chain. Paper 4, AI
Agents That Matter, names latency once as a desideratum and then measures only
dollars.

The three papers that do report absolute wall clock are all over a 1.5 second
budget before any tail effect is added. Paper 14, Zep, reports 2.58 and 3.20
second read path means, and never times its write path at all. Paper 16 reports
110 to 152 seconds per sample for precisely the detect-gap-then-fill task
Mytheus must run inside a 30 second window. Paper 11 reports 2.66 seconds for
the query expansion call alone and explicitly never mitigates it.

\begin{takeaway}
A p95 budget is a statement about the tail, and a mean says almost nothing
about it. Because the field publishes only means and ratios, there is no
published evidence that any of these retrieval, memory or attribution methods
can be served inside a bounded response time. That makes a quality, cost and
p95 frontier an empirical contribution rather than an engineering footnote.
\end{takeaway}

\subsection{Theme 2: one input, one reference}

No evaluation substrate in the corpus can measure whether two readers of the
same event should receive different output, because every benchmark binds one
input to exactly one reference answer.

Paper 10, LaMP, is the field's personalization benchmark and states it
outright: every input belongs to exactly one owning user, so divergence across
users on identical input is inexpressible. Paper 15, QMSum, is the sharpest
case, since its own claim is that one meeting yields different summaries for
different queries, yet each query is anonymous and pairs with exactly one gold
summary. Paper 17 scores every policy against a single human reference.

Paper 16 is the near miss and must be conceded honestly. It produces
reader-tailored meeting summaries from transcript-derived personas, which is
the closest published work to the Mytheus thesis. What it lacks is a per-reader
reference, so it cannot test whether the tailoring is real. Its own LLM judge
ranks the human gold summary lowest on informativeness and relevance, which is
itself evidence that the evaluation instrument, not the system, is the
bottleneck.

The mechanism side has the same shape. Every personalization method in the
corpus needs either a corpus the reader authored or a query the reader typed.
A live shared meeting supplies neither. Scoring relevance for a silent
participant over a jointly authored artefact is not a harder version of a
solved problem, it is a primitive none of these methods accept as input.

\subsection{Theme 3: coordination has no clock, and no principal}

Across every coordination framework in the corpus, termination is a logical
predicate, goal satisfied, iteration cap, message cap, reply count cap, and
never a wall clock. Paper 1 terminates on a satisfied condition or a cap on
consecutive auto replies. Paper 2 bounds cost with a 40 message cap and
enforces stopping through a prompt token the agent itself must decide to emit.
Paper 5 limits stopping to a maximum iteration count.

Paper 3 supplies the strongest evidence. Across 1642 traces from seven
frameworks it identifies fourteen failure modes, and not one of them is
temporal. Deadline miss, stale context under latency and partial result on
timeout are absent from the field's own failure vocabulary.

The second half of this theme is addressing. The two dominant topologies each
destroy the precondition for peer querying: broadcast eliminates the
information asymmetry that makes asking worthwhile, and hierarchy eliminates
lateral edges. Paper 1 broadcasts every response to all members and carries no
identity on the message. Paper 6 states plainly that its subagents never query
one another and that agents coordinate poorly in real time, which is the ideal
citation position, since it publishes the problem statement without the
mechanism.

\subsection{Theme 4: correct silence is unscorable}

Every evaluation instrument in the corpus either penalises abstention or
cannot see it. Paper 21, ALCE, is explicit: a statement with an empty citation
set scores citation recall zero, and a system that declines to answer emits no
statements to average over, so declining is strictly dominated. Paper 15
offers no abstention option. Paper 9 is strictly binary with no middle band.

Worse, paper 3 classifies information withholding as a pure defect, with no
category for a refusal that is correct under an authorization policy. A system
built to the published guidance is therefore optimised toward disclosure, and
Mytheus's deny-by-default scoping and its silent-on-empty rule would both be
scored as failures by the field's own taxonomy.

This matters more than it first appears. Two of the load-bearing Mytheus
invariants, the below-0.4 discard band and the silence terminal of the
ContextGapAgent fallback chain, are currently unreportable as successes. There
is no vocabulary in which the system can say it was right to stay quiet.

\subsection{Theme 5: grounding is established on clean written prose}

Not one paper measures confidence calibration or citation validity on spoken,
disfluent, diarized, partially erroneous transcript text. Paper 20 confines
itself to static single-turn written question answering plus one biography
task. Paper 21 defines citation validity as entailment over stable document
spans. Paper 18 fits a scalar on a held-out set drawn from the same
distribution and runs no shift experiment.

Two consequences follow. First, a fixed uniform threshold set by hand is not
defensible as a design claim: every paper that sets an operating point by hand
names that as a limitation, and every paper that fits one shows the fit does
not transfer across distributions. The Mytheus 0.7 and 0.4 cuts are currently
stipulated, not measured, and that is the most attackable number in the
design.

Second, citing a spoken utterance is undefined. Speaker attribution
correctness is not a component of citation validity in any published
attribution measure in this corpus, even though for a decision or an action
item the content of the claim is who committed to what.

\subsection{Theme 6: the write path, the tenant and the incidence of cost}

Memory and graph papers price the read path and leave the write path
unmeasured. Paper 14 reports query latency precisely and never measures
ingestion latency or the number of model calls per ingested message across its
five extraction stages. Paper 12 builds its hierarchy in one offline pass and
never measures adding one finished meeting. In a live meeting system the write
path is the continuous, latency bound, dominant cost, so architecture choices
made on these published numbers rest on the wrong side of the ledger.

Tenant isolation is not merely missing, it is a precondition violation. Paper
12 runs community detection over one undivided corpus, so communities would
span organisations by construction. Paper 13 lets entities bridge documents
with no entitlement boundary. Partitioning per organisation does not degrade
these methods gracefully, it invalidates the construction.

Finally, every cost result prices per input on the assumption of one output.
Nobody has modelled what per-recipient divergence does to caching, batching and
budget enforcement, nor reported the incidence of cost control: when the budget
binds, whose output degrades, and by how much. Mean quality hides this
entirely, and worst-participant quality is the number that actually decides
whether the product is usable.
